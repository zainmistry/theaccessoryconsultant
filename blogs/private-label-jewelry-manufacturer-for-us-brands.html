What private label jewelry manufacturing means for US brands

In B2B jewelry, private label usually means product manufactured for your brand rather than sold under the factory’s brand. You may supply CAD, sketches, or references; the factory develops production-ready files where needed, samples for approval, then produces the ordered quantity under your commercial terms.

Private label is not the same as buying from a catalog and sticking a hangtag on it. Catalog fill can work for some assortments, but private label is about control: silhouette, finish direction, stone choice, and the ability to restock the same SKU later without redesigning from scratch.

For US brands, private label programs typically need:

  • Clear ownership of design intent and brand presentation
  • Sampling before volume so the first shipment matches the lookbook and PDP photography
  • Material choices that fit US price architecture and return expectations
  • Confidentiality when the collection is unreleased or competitively sensitive
  • A factory path that supports both first capsules and later restocks

Individuals and small teams also use private label–style manufacturing when quantity fits a production model. Our MOQ starts from 50 units, so the engagement is still commercial manufacturing rather than one-off atelier work.

Who private label is for—and when it is not the right model

US brands and D2C labels are the core private-label buyers: they need branded product, often with restricted design sharing, and they care as much about sample approval and communication as about unit economics.

Wholesalers and retailers sometimes run private-label or exclusive lines for their own channels. The factory relationship still follows CAD → sample → production; the difference is merchandising and inventory planning.

Private label is a weaker fit when:

  • You only need a few units for a photoshoot and have no production volume path
  • You want the factory to invent an entire brand identity and assortment without a brief
  • You expect a fixed “ships free to US in X days” promise before destination and method are known

If you already own full technical specs and mainly need production to those specs, you may be closer to OEM. If you need heavier design development from concept, you may be closer to ODM. How a private label program runs: CAD → sample → production

Reliable private label work follows three stages. Skipping sampling to compress a US launch calendar is a common source of returns, remakes, and strained factory relationships.

CAD

CAD converts concept into a manufacturable 3D model. Proportions, stone seats, thicknesses, and findings logic get locked before metal and plating decisions become expensive to reverse. Brands with production-ready CAD can move faster; brands with sketches, mood boards, or competitor references start with modeling and revision rounds.

Good private-label CAD answers commercial questions early: will the earring post hold for daily wear, will the pendant bail support the stone layout, will the ring shank thickness survive casting and finishing at your quality tier?

Sample

The physical sample is the approval checkpoint for finish, weight feel, clasp function, stone appearance, and color under real light. Feedback should be specific and written. Approved samples become the QC reference for production—especially important when the brand team is in the US and the factory is in Mumbai.

Production

After sample approval and order confirmation, production runs to the agreed quantity and specification at our Mumbai factory. Consistency across units matters more than a single hero piece. Factory QC checks against the approved sample and written specs.

The process is documented on Process. Typical CAD-to-delivery is about 18 days, depending on design complexity, materials, stones, sampling revisions, and order size. That figure is factory-side manufacturing lead time. International shipping to the US is confirmed separately once destination and method are agreed—we do not publish fixed transit SLAs.

MOQ, timelines, and how US brands should plan a first capsule

Two planning anchors matter in almost every private-label conversation:

  • MOQ from 50 units
  • Approximately 18 days CAD-to-delivery

MOQ from 50 supports commercial runs while remaining accessible for many US D2C and early brand programs. Style count multiplies complexity: eight SKUs at 50 each is a different production and QC load than one SKU at 400. For a first capsule, prefer depth (more units per style) unless assortment breadth is the commercial priority.

~18-day CAD-to-delivery is a typical window when the brief is aligned. It can extend with complex designs, custom stones, multiple sample revisions, or larger volumes. Confirm timeline on the quote for your specific styles.

Build the US launch calendar backward from shelf or fulfillment date: factory CAD-to-delivery, plus your photography, packing, and shipping once destination is known. Do not assume ocean or air transit until method and destination are confirmed.

NDAs and confidentiality in private label programs

Private label often involves unreleased drops, influencer collaborations, or designs that competitors should not see early. We offer NDAs for private label and OEM engagements when confidentiality is required.

Practical approach for US brands:

  1. State early that designs are confidential.
  2. Share enough for preliminary feasibility, then full CAD after NDA if needed.
  3. Keep approved samples and final specs in a clear record—not only in chat threads.
  4. Include shipping country / US delivery destination in the same early packet so commercial and logistics questions travel together.

An NDA protects disclosure. Approved samples and written specs protect quality outcomes. Use both when the collection is sensitive.

What to send in a private label RFQ

Factories quote faster when the brief is complete. A useful first package includes:

  • Product types and approximate style count
  • CAD, sketches, or reference images
  • Preferred materials and stone direction
  • Target quantities (MOQ from 50)
  • Timeline targets for launch or restock
  • Confirmation that you need private-label terms and whether an NDA is required before full files
  • Shipping country / delivery destination (e.g. United States—city/state or port preference if known)
  • Company and contact details

How US brands reduce risk on a first private label order

  1. Start with fewer SKUs if the factory relationship is new. Prove finish and communication before expanding.
  2. Approve samples in writing. Ambiguity across time zones creates costly disputes.
  3. Separate design changes from production. Changes after approval are a new decision with timeline impact.
  4. Organize restock files. Keep CAD and approved sample references so reorder finish matches the first run.
  5. Match material to channel. Wrong tier creates margin or return problems even when manufacturing is correct.
  6. State destination early. “Ship to US” is a start; city/state or port preference helps logistics discussion without fake SLAs.
