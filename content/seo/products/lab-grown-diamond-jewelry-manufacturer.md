## What to specify for stones

Lab-grown diamonds vary in shape, size, colour and clarity just as natural diamonds do. In your brief, tell us:

- Shapes (round, oval, emerald, pear, princess and so on)
- Sizes or carat targets for centre and accent stones
- Your quality expectations and target retail price
- Whether a grading document is important for your marketing

Stone specifications, availability and pricing are confirmed per project in your quote. We do not publish grades, certifications or prices on this page.

## Tiering a collection with lab-grown stones

A common structure for lab-grown brands is three tiers. An entry tier uses small accent stones in sterling silver or vermeil, giving customers a real diamond at a gift price. A core tier uses solid gold with modest centre stones for everyday fine jewelry. A hero tier carries larger centre stones and bridal designs. Each tier can share design language, such as the same prong style or band profile, so the collection feels coherent. Because the minimum applies per design, you can start with one or two pieces per tier and expand as sales data comes in.

## Being clear with customers

US customers expect clear disclosure about whether a diamond is lab-grown or natural. Describe your stones accurately in your marketing and on your packaging. If you sell both, keep them clearly separated in your catalogue. We recommend checking product descriptions with your own advisers.

## How a lab-grown program runs

1. **Brief.** Designs, stone plan, metal and quantities per design.
2. **NDA.** For private label and OEM programs.
3. **CAD.** Models and renders. Check stone layout, setting style and how light will reach the stones.
4. **Sample.** A physical piece shipped to you. Check sparkle, setting security and finish in person.
5. **Production.** Manufactured and checked against the approved sample.
6. **Delivery.** Packed and shipped to your US address.
