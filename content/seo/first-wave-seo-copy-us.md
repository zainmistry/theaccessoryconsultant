Choosing a custom jewelry manufacturer is a commercial decision, not only a creative one. ## What “custom jewelry manufacturer” means for US B2B buyers

A custom jewelry manufacturer builds product to your design or agreed specification—not only from a fixed catalog. For US brands that usually implies:

- Design input via CAD or approved references  
- Sampling before volume production  
- Material and stone choices aligned to your price architecture and channel  
- Commercial terms around quantity, timeline, confidentiality, and delivery destination  

Sourcing from India, done well, means controlled production: repeatable quality, documented specs, and a path from first sample to restock—manufactured in Mumbai and shipped to your US warehouse, 3PL, or other agreed destination once method and destination are confirmed.

## Who typically sources custom jewelry manufacturing from India

Different buyers need different engagement models. The factory relationship works best when both sides are clear about ownership of design and how much development is required.

**US brands and D2C labels** often need private-label or custom collections that match brand identity. Confidentiality and sample approval matter as much as unit cost; many start with a small capsule at low MOQ, then expand after the first run proves finish and communication.

**Wholesalers and retailers** usually care about MOQ, restock planning, and batch consistency. State US delivery destination early in the RFQ.

**OEM buyers** supply full specs; **ODM buyers** need more development support—from concept and CAD through production-ready samples. **Individuals** may commission quantity production when the design meets MOQ.

Across segments, the question is the same: can the factory deliver the design in the chosen materials, at a sellable volume, in a workable timeline—and can shipping be scoped once destination is known?

## Engagement models: private label, OEM, custom, ODM

Clarity on the model prevents scope confusion later.

**Private label** means product manufactured for your brand, often with restricted sharing of designs. For private-label and OEM programs, we offer **NDAs** when confidentiality is required. Raise NDA needs early—ideally before exchanging full design files.

**OEM** means you own or supply the design and technical requirements; the factory manufactures to those requirements. Specs, tolerances, and approved samples become the contract language for quality.

**Custom manufacturing** means we produce to your design brief. You may arrive with sketches, CAD, or physical references. We develop or refine CAD, sample, then produce at our Mumbai factory.

**ODM** means you need design and manufacturing support. The factory helps move from concept to production-ready samples, then into volume. IP and commercial terms should be agreed up front so ownership of developed designs is unambiguous.

## The production path: CAD → sample → production

Most reliable factory programs follow three stages. Skipping sampling to “save time” often costs more in rejected goods or redesign mid-production—especially across countries.

### CAD
CAD turns a concept into a manufacturable 3D model—locking proportions, stone seats, thicknesses, and findings logic before metal is poured or plated. Brands with existing CAD can start further along; brands with sketches or photos start with modeling and revision rounds. Good CAD answers practical questions early: fit, bail strength, pave density, and production consistency at your quality tier.

### Sample
A physical sample is the approval checkpoint: finish, weight feel, clasp function, stone appearance, and look under real light. Feedback should be specific (“thicker post,” “warmer plating tone,” “lower stone height”). Approved samples become the QC reference; without them, quality disputes become likely—especially across time zones.

### Production
After sample approval and order confirmation, production runs to the agreed quantity and specification in Mumbai. Consistency across units matters more than a single hero piece. Factory QC checks work against the approved sample and written specs.

Our standard process is documented on [Process](/process). Typical **CAD-to-delivery is about 18 days**, depending on complexity, materials, stones, revisions, and order size. That figure is factory-side manufacturing lead time; international shipping to the US is confirmed separately once destination and method are agreed.

## Low MOQ and lead times: how US buyers should plan

Two numbers matter in almost every first conversation with a factory:

- **MOQ from 50 units**  
- **Approximately 18 days CAD-to-delivery**  

Treat both as planning anchors, not slogans.

**MOQ from 50** suits commercial runs while remaining accessible for many US D2C and early-stage brand programs. Style count multiplies complexity: ten SKUs at 50 each differs from one SKU at 500. For a capsule, choose depth (more units per style) or breadth (more styles at MOQ)—depth usually yields cleaner production and simpler QC.

**~18-day CAD-to-delivery** is a typical window for aligned programs. It can extend with complex designs, custom stones, multiple sample revisions, or large volumes—and moves more smoothly when CAD is production-ready and samples need few changes. Confirm timeline on the quote for your brief.

Build your US launch calendar backward from shelf or fulfillment date: factory CAD-to-delivery plus your own QC, photography, packing, and shipping. Do not assume a fixed ocean or air transit SLA until destination and method are confirmed.

If your designs are a competitive asset—seasonal drops, influencer collaborations, unreleased collections—ask for an NDA before sharing full CAD or high-resolution design packs. We offer NDAs for **private label and OEM** engagements.

Confidentiality does not replace clear specifications. An NDA protects disclosure; approved samples and written specs protect quality outcomes.

## How US brands reduce risk when working with an India factory

Experienced buyers treat the first order as a systems test, not only a product test.

1. **Start with fewer SKUs** if you are new to the factory relationship. Prove finish and communication before expanding the line.  
2. **Approve samples in writing** (email confirmation is enough). Ambiguity here creates costly disputes later—especially across time zones.  
3. **Separate design changes from production.** Changes after sample approval should be treated as a new decision with timeline and cost impact.  
4. **Plan restocks.** If a style sells in the US, you will want the same finish and construction again. Keep CAD and approved sample references organized.  
5. **Use the right engagement model.** Private label and OEM with NDA when designs are sensitive; ODM when you need development help; custom when you already know the design.  
6. **State destination early.** “Ship to US” is not a full logistics brief, but naming country (and city/state or port preference when known) lets the quote conversation include real shipping options without fake SLAs.

None of this requires hype. It requires process discipline—the same discipline that makes India manufacturing viable for serious US brands.
