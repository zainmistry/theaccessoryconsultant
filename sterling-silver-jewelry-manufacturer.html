Sterling silver jewelry manufacturer for US brands

Silver as a base for vermeil

Gold vermeil is gold over sterling silver, letting you sell a gold look with a precious-metal base. Many brands run silver and vermeil versions of the same design. Read our gold vermeil guide.

How a silver program runs

Brief. Designs, finishes, stones and quantity per design. Include your target retail price.

NDA. For private label and OEM programs, before files are shared.

CAD. Models and renders. For silver, check thickness: too thin and pieces bend, too thick and they become heavy and costly.

Sample. A physical sample ships to your US address.

Production. Manufactured and checked against the approved sample.

Delivery. Packed and shipped to your studio, store or 3PL.

Briefing silver weight and thickness

Silver pieces are priced and perceived largely by weight. If you have a target weight per piece, or a competitor piece you consider the right heft, tell us in the brief. We can model to a target in CAD and confirm it on the sample. Thin pieces keep prices down but can bend; heavier pieces feel premium but raise cost. Finding the balance for your price point is part of the CAD review, not something to discover after production. For rings, also confirm the band holds its shape across your size range.

Planning a silver range that grows

Brands that last usually build silver ranges in layers. Start with a handful of core designs that sell all year: a band, a hoop, a chain and a pendant. Add seasonal pieces around them, then introduce vermeil versions of your bestsellers once demand is clear. Because each design has its own minimum, you can extend the range one style at a time without reordering everything. Keep your approved samples as your reference library so every new batch of a core design matches the last.

Caring for silver: tell your customers

Sterling silver naturally tarnishes over time. Brands that include simple care instructions with each order see fewer complaints. Ask us about packaging that includes care cards in your brief.
